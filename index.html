UX Portfolio

Designing systems that make everyone better at what they do.

I'm a designer by training who learned to code, and I've spent my whole career paying attention to how people think and work together. That shapes how I work: the best outcomes happen when everyone contributes from their strengths. Because I can work well with everyone at the table, we can spend less time translating and more time moving forward.

I care about systems thinking and about creating design that makes people ask, "why didn't it always work this way?" I take a lean approach to problem solving: start with what you know, name your assumptions, and test as you go. Good work doesn't wait for perfect conditions.

How I think about UX

50+ talks given over 13 years

View my talks (opens in new tab)

I started my career as a visual communications designer who ended up in a room with developers, and it changed how I think about everything. Designers and developers didn't speak different languages. The processes we worked in usually kept us from talking at all. When we did talk, I realized we all wanted the same things. We just had different tools for getting there.

To solve a design problem instead of putting a visual bandage on it, you have to zoom out. Nothing happens in a vacuum. A content decision shapes what's possible in a design. A workflow decision shapes what's possible for a user interaction. A team dynamic shapes what's possible in shipping a product. I'm always looking for those connections and working out how to turn them into something consistent and useful for real people.

I draw from UX frameworks like Lean UX and from people like Jared Spool and the Nielsen Norman Group, but most of what shapes my practice comes from outside the field: researchers, therapists, philosophers, and educators. These are people who study how humans relate to each other and what it takes to get things done together. Over 13 years I've given more than 50 talks at open source conferences, mostly teaching design concepts to non-designers and technical concepts to non-technical people. I share what I know with people who are experts in their own fields, and I learn from them in return.

What I keep coming back to is mutual benefit. The best experiences (and the best processes) let everyone involved do what they came to do, whether they're users, clients, or colleagues. Pixels, typography, color, interfaces, and conversations are all tools. User experience is the thing underneath all of them, and it doesn't need any particular medium to exist.
